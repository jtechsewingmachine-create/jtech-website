## Original idea

PROJECT: Jtech sewing machine website

PURPOSE
[CONFIRMED] Create a premium, authentic, trustworthy and attractive product website for Jtech sewing machines. It should help visitors understand the products, choose a suitable machine and take a clear next step toward buying.

[CONFIRMED] This phase focuses on website architecture, user flows and wireframes, followed by polished visual design and a private working prototype. The business owner is currently unavailable, so work must proceed with clearly identified mock content and unresolved business details.

BUSINESS CONTEXT
[CONFIRMED — owner-reported] Jtech is my uncle’s sewing-machine brand. His business also sells Usha and Singer machines. A manufacturing partner supplies machines, while my uncle manufactures traditional sewing-machine tables. Do not describe him as manufacturing every machine component.

[CONFIRMED] Jtech already has a logo that should be preserved. The owner reports that the brand/logo is being registered; do not turn this into a verified legal claim.

[CONFIRMED] Usha and Singer are the two competitors selected for this website study. We reviewed 14 Usha screenshots and 30 Singer screenshots.

USER AND OUTCOME
[ASSUMPTION] The initial primary visitor is a household buyer who may not understand machine categories, stitch terminology or model differences.

[PROPOSED] Secondary visitors include experienced sewing users, existing owners seeking help and retailers or wholesale buyers. Keep these routes proportionate; do not build separate portals automatically.

[PROPOSED] Successful use means a visitor understands whether a machine fits their intended work, what is included, which features matter, what support is available and how to proceed.

VISUAL EXPERIENCE
[CONFIRMED] I want a product-led homepage with a prominent sewing machine, video and dynamic presentation inspired by premium consumer-product, car and motorcycle websites.

[PROPOSED] Explore a cinematic opening, subtle floating movement, controlled close-ups and scroll-based product storytelling. Motion should explain or showcase the product while keeping navigation and actions clear.

[PROPOSED] Provide a still-image fallback, reduced-motion experience and usable mobile layouts. Visitors must not need to watch an animation to browse products or contact the business.

[CONFIRMED] This engine should produce structural wireframes, not final visual styling. Preserve room for creative composition. Describe the purpose, content, actions and motion intent of each section without forcing the final UI into generic repeated boxes.

CURRENT SCOPE
[PROPOSED] Begin with the smallest coherent journey:
Homepage → machine listing → product detail → enquiry or purchase handoff.

[PROPOSED] Include basic support/contact and location discovery where useful. Learning content and an own-model comparison may be sections or lightweight routes rather than extensive standalone systems.

[PROPOSED] Wireframe the homepage and one representative product detail page first, within a lightweight overall navigation and flow map. Then refine listing and enquiry behavior.

[DEFERRED] Full customer accounts, cart and payment processing, coupons, wishlists, retailer portals, repair-partner dashboards and warranty-claim administration. These must not enter the immediate prototype automatically.

## Product model

**Problem:** Prospective Jtech customers may lack the product knowledge and verified information needed to distinguish machine types, understand model suitability and supplied configurations, assess support availability, and choose a reliable next step toward buying.

**Vision:** A premium, product-led Jtech website that presents sewing machines authentically, makes technical differences understandable, and builds trust through clear product, business, configuration, and support information.

**Primary outcome:** A visitor identifies a potentially suitable machine, understands the available verified information and unresolved conditions, and submits a model-specific enquiry as the prototype buying handoff.

**MVP boundary:** A responsive public website architecture covering homepage, compact machine catalogue, one representative product-detail pattern, short model-specific enquiry, basic support/contact information, and lightweight location discovery. The first wireframe pass should prioritize the homepage and one product detail page. Transactions and operational service systems remain outside the MVP.

## Requirements

- **Provide a compact product-discovery journey** [proposed] — The website must provide a clear route from the homepage to machine discovery, product details, and a next step toward buying.

- **Create a product-led homepage structure** [confirmed] — The homepage must prominently feature a sewing machine and accommodate video or dynamic product presentation without depending on motion for comprehension.

- **Explain product suitability** [proposed] — Product summaries and details should explain the intended work, relevant user needs, and important limitations in understandable language.

- **Make supplied configuration explicit** [proposed] — The website must distinguish the machine head from packages and list verified included components without inferring them from imagery.

- **Provide decision-complete product details** [proposed] — A product detail must organize media, suitability, contents, feature explanations, demonstrations, specifications, limitations, price status, learning, support, and relevant alternatives.

- **Support a model-specific enquiry** [proposed] — Until the sales channel is confirmed, the prototype should use a short enquiry that carries the selected model and configuration.

- **Represent support accurately** [proposed] — The website must provide clear support information while distinguishing sales, demonstrations, general repairs, and authorized warranty service.

- **Accommodate local service discovery** [confirmed] — The architecture must accommodate location-based support discovery because local service availability is an important business concern.

- **Use evidence-based trust content** [proposed] — Trust must be established through accurate product information, authentic business evidence, understandable policies, and accessible support rather than invented social proof.

- **Structure reusable product content** [proposed] — Product facts should be modeled consistently so verified data can drive cards, details, comparisons, tutorials, and later content generation.

- **Preserve reference-content identity** [proposed] — Competitor or generated materials used in private exploration must retain their identity and must not be represented as verified Jtech products or performance evidence.

- **Cover critical interface states** [proposed] — The structural design should cover loading, missing-media, empty-list, unavailable-product, invalid-input, submitting, failed-submission, and success states where relevant.

- **Preserve accessible interaction** [proposed] — Core browsing and enquiry interactions must use readable labels, keyboard-operable controls, visible focus, sufficient contrast, and explicit states without relying only on hover, color, or animation.

- **Keep catalogue controls proportionate** [deferred] — Filters and product comparison should be introduced only if the verified range is large or varied enough to justify them.

## Features

- **Compact public navigation** [proposed] — A lightweight navigation connecting machine discovery, choosing help, support or locations, and contact without creating separate portals.

- **Product-led homepage opening** [proposed] — A prominent product media block with concise context, media controls, a primary machine-discovery action, and still-image and reduced-motion alternatives.

- **Intended-use discovery** [proposed] — A small set of plain-language entry points that helps unfamiliar visitors browse by intended work or machine category.

- **Featured model distinctions** [proposed] — Homepage product summaries showing model identity, suitability, configuration, price status, and meaningful verified differences.

- **Demonstration and feature story** [proposed] — Semantic media and explanatory content that connects verified product features to practical outcomes, with fallback states.

- **Machine catalogue** [proposed] — A compact listing whose cards expose identity, imagery, suitability, configuration, useful differences, price status, availability status, and a detail action.

- **Decision-support product detail** [proposed] — A reusable detail structure covering product identity, media, suitability, configuration, contents, benefits, limitations, setup, specifications, demonstrations, price status, support, learning, and relevant Jtech alternatives.

- **Model-specific enquiry** [proposed] — A short simulated enquiry carrying the selected product and configuration, explaining the expected response and covering validation, submission, success, and failure.

- **Lightweight location discovery** [proposed] — A simple list or lookup of verified sales, demonstration, repair, and warranty-service locations, with fictional prototype entries clearly marked.

- **Contextual learning links** [proposed] — Lightweight buying guidance and tutorial links placed near browsing and product decisions rather than a large standalone content system.

- **Own-model comparison** [deferred] — A lightweight comparison of verified Jtech models if the confirmed range creates a real comparison need.

## Decisions

- **Which primary sales route should the production website support?** — [PROPOSED — PROTOTYPE ONLY] Use model-specific enquiry as the primary prototype route, carrying the selected model and configuration into the enquiry. Allow a secondary external-marketplace action as a candidate. The production sales route remains undecided until owner confirmation. Defer direct checkout infrastructure.

- **Which Jtech models, configurations, and intended buyer segments belong in the initial catalogue?** — [ASSUMPTION — PROTOTYPE ONLY] Use a small curated sample catalogue of three clearly labeled demo models to test browsing and comparison. Include one example with different package configurations to test how included components are explained. Start with household buyers; preserve trade/wholesale interest as a secondary enquiry intent. Actual models, categories, configurations and audiences remain unconfirmed. Do not invent a broad catalogue or complex filters.

- **What verified data is available for each launch model?** — [CONFIRMED — CURRENT KNOWLEDGE] No complete verified Jtech product dataset is available for this project yet. Use clearly labeled mock content during wireframing. Competitor material may serve as identified reference content, never as Jtech specifications. Actual capabilities, contents, limitations, origin and media require verification.

- **Will launch pages show prices, taxes, stock, delivery charges, and delivery coverage?** — [PROPOSED — PROTOTYPE ONLY] Include clearly labeled mock prices and configuration-specific price changes so we can design a useful buying experience. Reserve fields for tax treatment, stock, delivery charges and coverage, with unknown/unavailable states. Whether production displays exact prices, indicative prices or price on enquiry remains undecided. Mock values must not become production facts.

- **Who receives production enquiries, through which channel, and what response should the visitor expect?** — [ASSUMPTION — PROTOTYPE ONLY] Simulate phone or messaging follow-up. Collect a name, contact number, selected model/configuration and optional question. Request a pincode only when needed for a location-dependent enquiry. Do not send real messages. The production recipient, channel, routing and response time require owner confirmation; do not promise a response deadline.

- **What warranty, setup, repair, and local service coverage can Jtech currently verify?** — [CONFIRMED — CURRENT KNOWLEDGE] Warranty, setup assistance, repair arrangements and authorized service coverage have not yet been documented for this project. Design a support summary and general contact route using explicit placeholders. Preserve the proposed local repair-partner network as deferred work. Do not publish invented warranty durations or service promises.

- **Which languages are required at launch, and which verified tutorials exist in those languages?** — [ASSUMPTION — PROTOTYPE ONLY] Use English for the first wireframes and visual prototype. Keep the content structure ready for later localization. Kannada and Hindi are candidates, not confirmed launch requirements. No verified Jtech tutorial library has been supplied; use labeled media placeholders. Production languages and tutorial availability remain open.

- **How many verified sales or service locations will the initial website contain?** — [CONFIRMED — CURRENT KNOWLEDGE] No production-ready list of verified locations and their capabilities has been supplied. For the prototype, use a few clearly fictional locations to explore list/map presentation and distinctions between sales, demonstrations and service. Do not imply actual authorization, coverage or distances. Verify real locations before publication.

## Flows

### Discover a suitable machine and submit an enquiry

Goal: Explore a representative demo machine and complete a simulated enquiry while the production model-and-configuration handoff remains a requirement.

1. Understand the offer — Review the product-led opening, optionally read the illustrative interest guidance, and choose Explore machines or Explore all machines to browse the full demo range. (Recovery: If video is unavailable, fails, or motion is reduced, show the still-image fallback and preserve the same text and actions.)
2. Scan the demo catalogue — Review three clearly labeled demo models and open the representative product-detail pattern. (Recovery: If the catalogue is empty or cannot load, explain that product information is unavailable and offer the general enquiry route.)
3. Evaluate the representative machine — Review representative suitability, configuration, contents, features, limitations, setup, specifications, support placeholders, and demo alternatives. (Recovery: If media fails, retain text and fallback imagery. If the representative model is unavailable, disable misleading purchase language and offer alternatives or a general enquiry.)
4. Submit a simulated enquiry — Choose an enquiry intent, then enter name, contact number, and an optional question in the representative form. (Recovery: Identify invalid fields with text, preserve entered values, and allow correction. On simulated submission failure, retain all values and provide Retry or Contact support.)
5. Review simulated confirmation — Review the simulated completion state and continue browsing, or use the missing-context recovery controls when confirmation details are unavailable. (Recovery: If confirmation details are unavailable, offer routes back to the enquiry and the demo catalogue without asserting automatic context detection.)

### Evaluate technical suitability

Goal: Inspect representative capability, setup, limitation, and configuration information before using the simulated enquiry.

1. Browse models — Scan demo-model differences and open the representative detail pattern. (Recovery: When a technical fact is missing, show Pending verification rather than substituting an inferred value.)
2. Inspect details — Review representative specifications, configuration, included and excluded items, limitations, setup needs, demonstrations, and learning placeholders. (Recovery: If a specification or support term is unknown, provide an enquiry route; production must carry selected-model context, but this prototype does not establish that handoff.)
3. Request clarification — Submit a simulated question using the representative enquiry form. (Recovery: Preserve entered form values after validation or simulated submission failure; do not claim preservation of product context that was not transferred.)

### Find an appropriate support route

Goal: Distinguish available support purposes and find an appropriate contact or location route without assuming warranty coverage.

1. Review support guidance — Review sales, demonstration, repair and warranty guidance, then choose a contact route. (Recovery: If no verified route exists for the visitor's need, explain the limitation and offer general contact without promising service.)
2. Review location availability — Review clearly fictional prototype locations and the service types demonstrated by the pattern. (Recovery: If no location matches or location data is unavailable, return to general support contact and optionally collect a pincode in the enquiry.)
3. Contact for clarification — Open a support-oriented enquiry and provide contact details, optional pincode, and a question. (Recovery: On invalid input or simulated failure, preserve entries and allow retry or return to support guidance.)

### Submit a trade-interest enquiry

Goal: Review the public range and identify the enquiry as trade or wholesale interest.

1. Review the public range — Browse the same clearly labeled demo catalogue used by other visitors. (Recovery: If catalogue information is insufficient, use the direct Trade Enquiry navigation item, which enters the declared trade_enquiry state with enquiryIntent set to trade.)
2. Identify trade intent — Use the bound trade enquiry intent and submit the simulated form; no model selection is required for direct trade entry. (Recovery: Retain form data after validation or simulated submission failure and allow retry.)

## Open review findings

- **major: Catalogue-to-detail journey does not establish the selected machine** — For the private prototype, either implement a supported selected-product binding through listing, detail and enquiry, or explicitly redefine the journey as a representative generic-detail demonstration without claiming model-specific completion.

- **major: Homepage mobile state contains navigation without the homepage journey** — Treat mobile as a responsive layout of the default homepage with the mobile navigation added, and verify that the rendered mobile presentation retains the hero, both discovery actions, guidance and downstream routes without duplicating content.

- **medium: Confirmation has no recovery content for missing context** — Add a concise missing-context state using existing enquiry recovery: explain that no confirmation details are available and provide a route back to screen_enquiry or safe continuation to Machines.

- **medium: Support flow describes a purpose selection that the screen does not provide** — Align the flow wording with the implemented generic guidance-and-contact journey, or later add supported topic selection only if routing by support purpose becomes necessary.

- **minor: Machines breadcrumb reuses a differently named shared action** — Runtime-check the breadcrumb label and accessible name. If the shared transition label is exposed, use a dedicated Machines breadcrumb action and transition while preserving the existing alternatives control.

- **major: Product detail is fragmented into too many empty sections** — For the private-prototype presentation, consolidate related pending material into fewer decision-oriented groups, such as suitability and specifications, package and setup, and support and learning. Retain the underlying content fields for later verified data without requiring every field group to appear as a separate prominent section now.

- **medium: The required simulation acknowledgement duplicates the form notice** — Keep the persistent prototype notice and remove the required acknowledgement checkbox unless explicit consent testing is a stated research objective. Production consent requirements remain unresolved and should not be represented by this simulation-specific field.

- **medium: The enquiry form asks for pincode before it is demonstrably needed** — For the smallest reliable prototype, omit pincode from the general form and let visitors include location details in the optional question. Add a dedicated conditional field only after the triggering context is represented and verified.

- **medium: Fictional map content adds premature complexity to location discovery** — Remove the map placeholder from the immediate private-prototype presentation. Retain the location list, fictional-data disclosure and enquiry recovery; revisit a map only when verified locations and a geographic user need exist.

- **minor: Global navigation gives secondary trade intent disproportionate prominence** — Keep one general Contact or Enquiry navigation entry and let trade visitors select Trade enquiry within the shared form. Retain the dedicated shortcut only if evidence shows trade traffic or business priority warrants persistent global prominence.

- **major: Product breadcrumb exposes conflicting control names and lacks confirmed breadcrumb semantics** — Give the breadcrumb’s Machines control one consistent accessible name without silently changing other controls that share action_view_alternative. In production, implement it as a labeled breadcrumb navigation region with Home and Machines links and a programmatically identified non-linked current page.

- **major: Choosing Help focus management is documented but not demonstrated** — Implement and keyboard-test focus transfer to a programmatically focusable guidance heading for both same-page and cross-page entry. Verify visible focus, sticky-header clearance, mobile-menu closure, and immediate movement under reduced motion.

- **medium: Cinematic media blocks do not contain concrete non-visual alternatives** — Before real media is introduced, supply asset-specific alternative text and a concise transcript or equivalent description of every informational demonstration. Keep decorative imagery explicitly decorative and ensure all actions and product context remain outside the media.

- **medium: Reduced-motion equivalence is represented but not verified** — Verify with prefers-reduced-motion enabled that cinematic motion and smooth scrolling do not start automatically, the still-first presentation is used, and all text and actions remain equivalent. Keep Play and Pause keyboard operable if motion can be started manually.

- **medium: Page heading hierarchy is planning metadata rather than verified semantics** — During implementation, map each planned H1 to an actual semantic heading, retain logical H2 ordering, and inspect the accessibility tree on all seven routes. Do not infer heading levels from block size, type, or visual styling.

- **medium: Visible focus, contrast, and target-size requirements have no project-specific evidence** — Treat these as mandatory visual-design and implementation acceptance checks: verify WCAG contrast, clearly visible focus in every state, keyboard reachability, non-color error/selection cues, and appropriately sized pointer targets on desktop and mobile.

- **major: Core catalogue-to-enquiry context handoff is not executable** — Define and verify one supported product/configuration data-binding contract before treating this journey as model-specific. Until then, keep the detail and enquiry explicitly generic and revise flow completion claims accordingly.

- **medium: Current revision has no project-specific journey execution evidence** — Run and record focused revision-82 checks for homepage-to-catalogue navigation, detail-to-enquiry navigation, trade handoff, simulated failure/Retry/success, support navigation, location recovery, and breadcrumb controls.

- **major: Mobile content composition is not represented by the saved contract** — Verify the mobile preview at revision 82. If state filtering hides the default content, implement viewport-owned responsive composition so the same semantic homepage content is reused with mobile navigation, without duplicating blocks.

- **medium: Several data-dependent screens lack explicit load-failure recovery contracts** — For each production data source, define the minimum transient-failure behavior using existing journeys: preserve navigation, distinguish unavailable data from an empty catalogue, and provide retry or a general enquiry/support route. Do not add offline-first infrastructure unless business needs justify it.

- **major: Reusable product content has no implementation source or publication workflow** — Before production implementation, select one authoritative product-data source and define field-level verification status, editor/approver permissions, publication rules, and how shared facts are consumed by listing, detail, enquiry, support, and location surfaces.

- **major: A live enquiry implementation lacks privacy and operational dependencies** — Keep the current prototype strictly simulated. Before enabling live submission, resolve the operational owner and channel, minimize collected data, define consent and retention handling, restrict staff access, add abuse controls, and verify delivery and failure monitoring.
